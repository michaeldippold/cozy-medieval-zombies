# Sprite definitions: the loader's field reference

**Status, 2026-10-16: a reference, written by the sweep from `src/assets.js` and the files that read a definition (`map.js`, `interact.js`, `ground.js`, `placement.js`, `stations.js`, `render/`), checked field by field against the 137 definitions in `data/sprites/`; the plumbing that first built the drawn-art paths is `history/17-village-art-build.md`.** Every picture the game draws is a sprite definition, `data/sprites/<id>.json`, read by `assets.loadSprite` into a sheet (an image, named frames, an anchor, animations) that has the same shape whether the picture is drawn, generated, or built from several files; the world names a definition by its id (a map's prop or floor, a wall material, a scatter rule, a station). A delivery of art is written into this format and nothing else: a definition and its PNGs under `data/art/<area>/`, listed by `data/manifest.json` (`tools/manifest.py`, regenerated by `serve.py` on start and after a save) and loaded up front at boot (`assets.loadAll`), so a hard refresh sees a new file by its stamp. The sprite tool (`tools/sprites.html`) edits a definition's pixels and anchors and saves it back; a save reaches a running game through `BroadcastChannel("cmz")` (`assets.reloadSheet`). Items' art (the held picture, the bag icon, the portrait) is on the item in `data/items.json`, at the end of this page. Every field below is read by the code named; `note` is read by nobody and is where a definition says what it is.

## The picture: one of four shapes

- **`image`, `frames`, `anchor`** (and `bounds`, `scale`, `smooth`): one PNG and frames as `[x, y, w, h]` rectangles into it (`idle_0` for a still thing); the trees, rocks, mushrooms, posts. `bounds` `{ x, y, w, h }` is the visible extent, for occlusion and hit tests. `scale` resamples the picture once at load (smooth unless `smooth: false`) so the world still draws it 1:1; no definition uses it today, since the trees were redrawn at size (`15b`), and art is never scaled by the artist or by `drawImage` (`docs/art/01`, rule 6).
- **`images`** (and `frames`, `anchors`, `anchorVertex`): `{ "<frameName>": "<path>" }`, several PNGs packed side by side into one canvas, each name a frame; `frames` given too add or override; `anchors` `{ "<frameName>": [x, y] }` gives an anchor per frame where they differ (the wall parts, the boards). The floors (`idle_0..3`, picked by hash in the ground bake), the walls (ten frames, `north_plain` to `west_window_open`), the wall parts (`leaf_<side>`, `leaf_far_<side>`, `shutter_<left|right>_<side>`, `shutter_far_<left|right>_<side>`), the boards (`boards_<door|window>_<side>_<1|2|3>`), the well (`idle_0`, `crank_1`, `crank_2`), the jack-o'-lantern (`idle_<facing>_<i>` with `flame_<facing>_<i>` over it), and the crops (`idle_x_0`, `idle_y_0`).
- **`drawings`, `facings`** (and `facingFootprint`, `fire`): furniture. Each facing is `{ "image", "anchor" }` or `{ "from": "<facing>", "mirror": true }`, a mirror made at load (never a display scale); a facing whose `facingFootprint` is two tiles is sliced per tile along a vertical screen line at the second tile's centre, moved 9 px toward the first (`SLICE_INSET`, half the thickest wall, so a far wall never draws over the second tile's high parts), each slice with its own anchor; frames are `idle_<facing>_<i>`. The bed, the table, and the fireplace lie along y as drawn and along x as mirrors; the chair's four facings (`n`, `e`, `s`, `w`, where the seat faces) are two drawings and their mirrors. The accepted cost of a mirror is that the lit side swaps (`docs/art/01`, rule 2).
- **`placeholder`** `{ "kind", ... }`: generated at boot by `placeholders.js` through the same interface, so nothing waits for art (`CLAUDE.md`). The kinds: `character` (`variant` `player` or `zombie`: the figure, `12`), `box` (`color`, `height`, `inset`, `label` drawn on it, `outline`), `post`, `tree` (`width`, `height`, `color`, `trunk`; `fruit` a colour and `fruitCount` dots of it in the canopy, a fruit tree laden), `flat` (`color`, `outline`), `wall` (`height`, `thickness`, `color`), `pumpkin`, `hearth`, `campfire`, `torch_post`, `flame`, `ground` (`variant` `dirt` or `cobble`, `width`, `height`), `noise` (`size`), `ui` (the kit: `stone`, `cut`, `timber`, `moss`, with `slices` naming each picture's corner). The sub-fields the generators read beyond those: `flat.alt`, a second colour for a two-frame flat (`placeholders.js:28`); `ground`'s `gap`, `top`, and `bottom` (the cobble's mortar, lit top, and dark underside) and `light` and `dark` (the dirt's grains, the water's ripple and trough); and `character.look`, which the loader passes to the figure (`assets.js:124`) and no definition sets. Drawn art replaces a generated sprite by swapping `placeholder` for the drawn shape in the same file; the sprite tool does it in one save (`data/art/generated/<id>.png`).

Headless (`tools/world.js`, CI), a sheet is its frames, anchors, animations, and facings with no picture: what the simulation reads and nothing the renderer would.

## Anchors

- **The engine's convention:** the anchor is the pixel of the frame drawn at the **tile's centre** on the ground. The pumpkins, the trees (measured four rows above the trunk's lowest pixel), the rocks, the decals' upright ones (`"stand": true` in `data/ground.json`) are given this way.
- **`anchorVertex: "top"`:** the anchors are given at the **tile's top vertex at ground level**, Claude Design's convention for walls, parts, boards, furniture, crops, and floors; the loader adds 16 px to y (`toCentreAnchor`). Both wall sides anchor on the top vertex. A post is anchored on its own centre at ground and drawn at the grid vertex where walls meet, no conversion.
- **A delivery's `anchors_*.json`** is copied into the definitions by an import script and never read by the game; `anchor_px`, `fire_base_px`, and `ground_anchor_px` are its names for the same points (`docs/art/01`, "Anchors and naming").

## Frames, facings, and animation

- **Frame names:** `idle_<i>` for a still prop (a multi-tile generated box gets one per footprint tile); `idle_<facing>_<i>` when the definition has `facings`; `flame_<i>` or `flame_<facing>_<k>` for the unlit frames drawn over a fire's body; anything a `clickAnim` names.
- **`facings`:** a list; the prop carries `facing` (the map's, else the first; `R` cycles it in placement), and `assets.idleFrame` picks `idle_<facing>_<i>`. For a two-drawing thing the facings are drawings, not mirrors (the jack-o'-lantern's `left` and `right`), so `R` turns one. `mirror` on a definition is the characters' table of mirrored facings; props mirror through `drawings`.
- **`facingFootprint`:** `{ "<facing>": [w, h] }`; the facing sets the footprint's axis, `R` on a carried piece swaps both together, and a map that gives a footprint and no facing gets the facing that fits.
- **`animations`:** `{ "idle": { "fps", "loop", "frames" } }`, the idle frames played by time (the jack-o'-lantern's three); a one-tile prop's `flame_<same index>` is drawn unlit over each. The characters' animations come from the figure, not the file.
- **`clickAnim`:** `{ "frames": [...], "ms": [...] }`, played once on a left click within reach and when an action starts (the well's bucket: part down, down, part down, up); a sprite with a `clickAnim` never sways.
- **`furrows`:** on a floor sheet, `"x"` or `"y"`, the direction its furrows run (`floor_farm`, `floor_farm_y`); on a crop, `true`, so `loadMap` sets the crop's facing from the floor under it once the floors are laid, unless the map gave one.

## What the world reads off a definition

A prop is created from a map entry and its definition (`map.createProp`); these fields decide what it is.

- **`interactable`** (Michael's law, `CLAUDE.md`): a game object with a gathering action, now or later: a click within reach sways it, its art has a dark outline all the way round, and a character walking through one that is not solid sets it swaying. The trees (excused from the outline), rocks, mushrooms, crops, and pumpkins. **`sway: false`** keeps the click from swaying an interactable (none uses it today; a `clickAnim` has the same effect).
- **`solid` is not a definition's field:** it is the map entry's (`props[].solid`, default true) or the scatter rule's (`solid: false` for mushrooms and crops), as `opaque` is. **`collide: { "radius" }`**, in tiles, makes a one-tile solid prop block a circle at its tile centre instead of the whole tile (the trees 0.18, 0.22, and 0.3 by tier; the rocks 0.38), so a body brushes past a trunk while the tile stays unwalkable for pathing.
- **`carry`:** `true` or `false` overrides the rule for whether a prop can be lifted and carried (`placement.canCarry`); without it, a prop is carried unless it has `wood`, `water`, or a fire that lights a room, holds a torch, or burns fuel.
- **`container: { "loot" }`:** a loot table in `data/loot.json`; `Search`, then `Open`, a bag rolled on first search (`interact.containerActions`).
- **`pick`:** `{ "item", "count": [lo, hi], "days", "seconds", "bare" }`, a fruit tree that can be picked by hand (`fruit.js`, #190): the item and a count rolled in the range go to the bag, the tree becomes its `bare` kind and bears again `days` of the clock later; **`ripens`** on the bare kind names the sprite it grows back to. Both kinds carry the same `wood`, `canopy`, and `leaves`.
- **`wood`:** how many logs `Chop down` yields; **`water`:** the well's actions (drink, fill); **`sleep`:** a bed; **`station`:** the station's name, which recipes in `data/recipes.json` are filed under (`stations.js`); **`walk`** and **`drink`** on a floor: `walk: false` is water no body walks, a number a speed factor (`floor_water_shallow`, 0.5), `drink: true` lets you drink and fill beside it.
- **`fire`:** `{ "radius", "flicker": { "amp", "speed" }, "room", "burns", "cooks", "maxFuel", "fuel", "hoursPerUnit", "lit", "holds" }` (`07`): the light's radius and flicker; `room` lights its whole room; `burns` and `cooks` with `fuel` and `hoursPerUnit` make it a hearth or campfire that takes logs; `lit` starts it burning (the jack-o'-lantern); `holds` is a bracket or post that takes a torch. On a furniture definition `fire.at` is the firebox's pixel (converted like the anchor), `fire.frames` the flame PNGs, `fire.base` the pixel of each flame that lands on it (default `[9, 15]`), `fire.fps` their rate (default 6); the loader composes `flame_<facing>_<k>` on the footprint tile that holds the firebox.
- **Trees:** `canopy: { "reach" }` (2, 3, 4 by tier: the ground bake's leaf and darkening reach; a definition with a `canopy` is what opens the see-through hole round the player, `CLAUDE.md`), `shadow: { "width" }` (a fraction of the frame's width; any prop with `shadow` casts a baked ellipse), `leaves` (`brown`, `yellow`, `orange`, `red`: the colour of the leaves under it).
- **Walls:** `wall: { "height", "thickness", "color", "material", "post", "parts", "boards", "seeThrough", "fence" }`: the face height and thickness (80; stone 16, wood 14, log 18), `post` the post definition, `parts` the leaves and shutters sheet, `boards` the overlays (`wall_boards` by default), `seeThrough: true` a fence or bars that sight and light pass and that bounds no room (`src/CLAUDE.md`). There is no `climb`: nobody climbs through a window (#134).
- **`slices`:** the UI kit's 9-slice corners per picture, in interface pixels (`src/ui/kit.js`).

## What a delivery looks like (the conventions since the village set, 2026-10-11)

Every PNG is hard-pixelled (alpha 0 or 255), tightly cropped, 1:1, and comes with its anchor in the delivery's JSON. Orientation matches the engine: **north** is the tile edge from its top vertex to its right vertex (`\` on screen, a room's back-right wall), **west** from the top vertex to the left (`/`, the back-left wall); nothing is mirrored between the two. A wall segment is one tile edge long and 80 px tall, centred on the edge line, with its cap and its near end face in the sprite (the cap is redrawn from the same picture with the sky's light, `render/walls.js`), five variants per side (`plain`, `door_shut`, `door_open`, `window_shut`, `window_open`); the doorway is 20 px across and 48 tall, the window 16 across with its sill at 22 and top at 51; the open leaf and the shutters are separate sprites drawn at the wall's anchor after the wall on the near side, and the `_far` ones before it only through a translucent wall; boards are overlays, one set for every material. Furniture is drawn with its long side along world y and sliced and mirrored by the loader; flames are 18 by 16 unlit frames placed by their base pixel. Floors are 64 by 32 diamonds patterned in world space, so any variant meets any other. **The outline is by kind:** props and items carry `#2b2522` (the interactable outline, `docs/art/01`, rule 4); a character part's, where it stands off the body, is the figure's own `#1a1420` (`src/figure/rig.js`, `28`); both are right. File names are `<thing>_<variant>_<orientation or index>.png`, lower case. The style and the scale reference are `docs/art/01`; how a delivery is checked and landed is `27`.

## Items' art (`data/items.json`)

An item's pictures are fields on its row, read by `main.js`'s `held` boot stage, `figure/held.js`, `render/entities.js`, and `ui/inventory-panel.js`.

- **`held`, generated:** `{ "shape", "colours", "swing", "size", "pose" }`: `figure.generateHeld` draws the shape (`torch`, `sword`, `halberd`, `bow`, `shield`, and the rest in `figure/held.js`) in its colours at sixteen angles, `size` the frame (32 by default; the halberd's 128), `swing: false` for a thing that does not swing with the arm (the shield, the staff, a log), `pose` the hold (`bow_rest`, `present`). The sheet is `held_<id>`, and the raised guard is `held_shield_guard`.
- **`held`, drawn:** `{ "mode", "image", "grip" }` or `{ "mode": "angles", "angles": [{ "src", "grip" }] }`, built by `assets.heldSheetFromImage`; `grip` is the pixel that sits on the hand, from the PNG's top-left. **Three modes:** `fixed` shows the one upright picture at every angle, unrotated, glued to the hand (the book); `rotate` turns it into the sixteen angles about its grip by nearest pixel (the knife and axe, 5 and 9 px wide, where that is acceptable); `angles` takes hand-drawn tilts at 0, 22.5, 45, 67.5, and 90 degrees clockwise from up and makes the rest by flips (the staff, and the model for every long weapon: a long weapon is asked for as angles). **The flip rule** (the loader's, matching `docs/art/07`): 90 to 180 is the drawn tilt at `180 − angle` flipped vertically (grip y mirrored); 180 to 270 the tilt at `angle − 180` flipped both ways (grip x and y mirrored); 270 to 360 the tilt at `360 − angle` flipped horizontally (grip x mirrored). Flips put the light on the wrong side at the mirrored angles, and nobody notices at 1x. A drawn `held` replaces the generated picture with no other change.
- **`icon`:** `{ "src", "size" }`, the bag's picture on a canvas of the item's exact footprint at 19 px a cell (a 1 by 4 item is 19 by 76), drawn upright on a dark slot at the largest whole enlargement that fits (2x); an item without one shows a block in its `color` with a text label. A stack's count is drawn by the bag in the cell's lower right, and a worn weapon's durability as a thin bar, never in the art.
- **`portrait`:** `{ "src", "size" }`, a square 38 by 38 picture of the thing as a thing, for the character screen's slots and the belt; until one comes, a square icon stands in (`inventory-panel.js`, `portraitOf`). None has been delivered yet; every item that can be held or worn owes one, with its world picture and its icon (`CLAUDE.md`, "Item art comes as a pair"; the backlog is `26`).
