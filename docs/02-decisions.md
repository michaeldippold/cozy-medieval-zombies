### 2026-10-13 — Sleep in a world with friends: time skips only when everyone is in bed (Michael)

**The ruling (Michael):** in a multiplayer world sleep is possible and optional, and both players must be in bed asleep before the time jump starts. **Before:** any one player lying down set the whole world's time scale to 60 (`startSleep`), so a friend still awake had their game fast-forwarded under them, and `wake` set it back to 1 even if another was still asleep. **Now:** the time scale is the world's, computed from who is asleep (`game.timeScaleNow`): the sleep speed only while every living player in the world is asleep, ordinary time otherwise. A dormant player is out of the world's list and a dead one is skipped, so neither blocks nor forces a skip. It is evaluated each step in `game.step` (a guest who joins, leaves, wakes, or dies puts the speed right) and at the moment of lying down and waking (`player.js` takes the rule from `game.js` by `setSleepScaleRule`, so a lone player still gets the skip at once, as `check("sleep")` expects). A sleeper alone in bed while a friend is awake sleeps at ordinary speed, rested and regenerating at the sleeping rate, and is told "Time will pass faster once everyone in the world is asleep." (a lone player or the last one in bed still hears "The night goes on without you."). A sleeper is still never woken by anyone else's doing: they get up when rested and it is morning, and then the world slows for any still asleep. **Verified:** `check("sleep")` 9 of 9, three new rows with two players on one bed (one asleep and one awake: scale 1; both: 60; one wakes: back to 1); the other lone-world checks unchanged (determinism `d9a6eb31`, players 9, save 7, craft 9, wear 4, growth 9, water 8, sway 8, worlds 10), headless the same. **For the multiplayer branch:** the frame rate to a guest must be paced by real time, not by steps, because while the world is at 60 the host runs up to 120 steps per browser frame (the plan for item 1 of the bandwidth work).

### 2026-10-12 — The knockdown is the game's crit (Michael's finding, `14`)

Michael: a sword knockdown on the first swing makes the follow-up a two-shot, "it feels like a crit without actually being one." **Measured (120 kills of a fresh 100-hp dead one per weapon, real swings, 2026-10-12):** sword 2 swings 32 percent of the time and 3 otherwise (mean 2.68, 1.34 s); mace 2 swings 67 percent (mean 2.33, 1.52 s); hatchet 3 or 4 (mean 3.5, 1.93 s); spear stab 3 or 4 (3.55, 2.13 s); stick 5 or 6 (5.5, 2.75 s). **The rule behind it:** every hit has a knockdown chance (0.3; the mace 0.6); a hit on a downed one is a finisher at twice the damage; so a first-hit knockdown kills in two when `damage + 2 x damage >= 100`, which is `damage >= 34`: the sword (34) and the mace (38) have it, the hatchet (30), spear (30) and stick do not. **It is emergent, not designed:** the sword clears the line by two hit points (34 + 68 = 102 against a 100-hp dead one; at 33 it vanishes), and the dead's hit points moving, a tired swing (half damage), or a guarded one (35 percent) all break it. **Ruled: keep it, and call the knockdown the crit**: the chance is visible (the dead one hits the floor) rather than a hidden roll on damage, which is why it feels fair. What to decide only if the sword proves too strong overall: the sword's knockdown chance (0.3), its damage by one point, or its stamina; and if the dead gain varied hit points, whether the two-shot line should be a stated rule (a `heavy` weapon tier) instead of an accident of 34.
